# A walkie talkie app that works without internet

Many walkie talkie apps send your voice over the internet, so they stop when the signal does. AirGrid sends voice directly between nearby Android phones over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Direct, so it keeps working with no internet or mobile data.

By Jayarous Antony KS, developer of AirGrid. Published 17 November 2026.

## Three ways to talk

- Walkie clips: send short voice clips to everyone on the local mesh, or start an invited private walkie session with one person.
- Rider Mode: a live one-to-one audio session with a trusted contact, with mute controls and a voice-activated microphone.
- Voice notes: record a message and send it in a chat.

## Set it up

1. Install AirGrid from Google Play on each Android phone while you are online.
2. Open it and allow the permissions it asks for, including the microphone.
3. When you are near each other, open AirGrid so the phones discover each other.
4. Mark the people you trust as trusted contacts to use private walkie and Rider Mode with them.

## How it compares with a radio

- A dedicated two-way radio reaches much further. AirGrid is for people close by.
- Range depends on the phones, the Android version, distance, radio conditions and the number of people nearby.
- It needs no extra hardware: only Android phones with the app installed.

## Common questions

### Does AirGrid's walkie talkie need internet?

No. Voice goes directly between nearby phones over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Direct.

### Can a whole group hear me?

Walkie clips can go to everyone on the local mesh. Private walkie sessions and Rider Mode are with one selected person.

### How far does it reach?

It varies. Performance depends on the phones, the Android version, distance, radio conditions and how many people are nearby.
