AirGrid, a chat app that works without internet

AirGrid is an offline-first chat app for Android. Nearby phones exchange messages directly over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Direct, using Google Nearby Connections, without internet, mobile data, routers or a central messaging server.

What AirGrid does

Messaging

Offline nearby messaging
Chat with nearby Android devices without internet access or mobile data.
Local mesh
Messages can move between connected nearby devices over a local peer-to-peer mesh.
Public nearby chat
Send messages to the people around you who are connected to the same local mesh.
Private chat
Message selected nearby contacts directly when they are available in the mesh.
Encrypted private messages
AirGrid supports opportunistic encryption for private messages once devices have exchanged the required public keys.

Voice, media and location

Voice notes and media sharing
Send voice notes, photos and files to nearby peers when the local connection supports the transfer.
Walkie-talkie audio
Send short public walkie clips to the local mesh, or start an invited private walkie session with one person.
Rider Mode
A trusted one-to-one live audio session with mute controls and a voice-activated microphone, for close-range coordination.
Optional location sharing
Share your location with connected peers only when you choose to.

Contacts and history

Known and trusted contacts
AirGrid can remember contacts discovered through the mesh. Mark trusted contacts to use private walkie and Rider Mode with them.
Local message history
Messages are stored on your device, so you can read recent conversations locally.

When AirGrid is useful

  • Events and community gatherings
  • Travel, hiking and camping
  • Campuses, dormitories, hostels and hotels
  • Workplaces where people are close by
  • Emergency preparedness, when nearby people need to talk without internet or mobile data

How AirGrid handles your messages

  • Messages are exchanged directly with nearby devices.
  • AirGrid does not operate a central messaging server for normal mesh messaging.
  • Message history is stored on your device, not on an AirGrid server.

Good to know

Google Play Services is required. AirGrid needs nearby-device permissions such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, notifications, microphone and location-related permissions, because Android requires them for nearby discovery, device-to-device connections, foreground mesh sessions and the optional audio and location features.

Mesh performance varies with device hardware, Android version, distance, radio conditions and the number of nearby peers.

Questions about AirGrid

Does AirGrid need internet or mobile data?
No. AirGrid sends messages directly between nearby Android devices over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Direct, without internet, mobile data or a router.
How far apart can AirGrid users be?
It depends. Mesh performance varies with device hardware, Android version, distance, radio conditions and how many peers are nearby. Messages can also hop between connected devices in the local mesh.
Are AirGrid messages encrypted?
Private messages use opportunistic encryption once the two devices have exchanged the required public keys.
What does AirGrid need to work?
An Android device with Google Play Services, plus nearby-device permissions such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, notifications, microphone and location-related permissions.
Where are AirGrid messages stored?
On your device. AirGrid does not operate a central messaging server for normal mesh messaging.
Can I send photos and voice notes with AirGrid?
Yes. You can send voice notes, photos and files to nearby peers when the local mesh connection supports the transfer.
